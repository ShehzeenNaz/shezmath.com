Work out how many drywall sheets a room needs, plus screws and joint tape. Enter the wall area and sheet size.

How It Works

Formula: Sheets = wall area (sq ft) ÷ area per sheet (sq ft) × 1.05 for waste. Screws = sheets × 32. Tape rolls = area ÷ 250 sq ft per roll.

Worked example: A wall 12 ft by 8 ft with 4 by 8 ft sheets: area is 96 sq ft. 96 ÷ 32 = 3 sheets. With 5% waste: 3 × 1.05 = 3.15, so 4 sheets. Screws: 4 × 32 = 128. Tape: 96 ÷ 250 = 0.38, so 1 roll.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many drywall sheets do I need?

Divide the wall area by the sheet area and add 5% waste. A 12 by 8 ft wall needs 4 sheets of 4 by 8 ft drywall.

What size drywall sheet should I use?

Four by eight foot sheets suit most rooms. Twelve foot sheets mean fewer joints on long walls but need two people to lift.

How many screws per drywall sheet?

About 32 screws per 4 by 8 ft sheet when studs are 16 inches apart. Use more on ceilings.

How much joint tape do I need?

One 250 foot roll of tape covers about 250 square feet of wall. Buy one roll per room for most jobs.

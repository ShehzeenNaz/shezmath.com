Stair Stringer Calculator

Work out the steps, riser height, total run and stringer length for a staircase. Keeps every step the same height, as building rules require.

Your numbers never leave your device. All calculations happen in your browser.

How It Works

Formula: Steps = total rise ÷ target riser height, rounded to a whole number. Actual riser = total rise ÷ steps. Total run = steps × tread depth. Stringer length = √(rise² + run²).

Worked example: A total rise of 36 in with a 7 in target riser: 36 ÷ 7 = 5.14, so 5 risers. Actual riser is 36 ÷ 5 = 7.2 in. With 11 in treads, the run is 5 × 11 = 55 in. Stringer length is √(36² + 55²) = 65.7 in.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good riser height?

Between 7 and 7.5 inches is comfortable for most people. Building rules usually cap risers at 7.75 inches.

How deep should stair treads be?

At least 10 inches, and 11 inches feels best. Shallower treads make stairs feel steep and unsafe.

Why must all risers be the same height?

Uneven risers cause trips. Every riser in one flight must match within a small tolerance, usually 3/8 inch.

How many stringers do I need?

Use two stringers for stairs up to 36 inches wide and three for wider stairs, so the treads do not flex.

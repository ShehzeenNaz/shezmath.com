Decking Calculator

How It Works

Formula: Rows of boards = deck width in inches ÷ (board width + gap). Total linear feet = rows × deck length. Boards = linear feet ÷ board length, rounded up.

Worked example: A deck 16 ft by 12 ft with 5.5 in boards and a 0.25 in gap: width is 144 in. 144 ÷ 5.75 = 25.04, so 26 rows. Linear feet: 26 × 16 = 416 ft. With 12 ft boards: 416 ÷ 12 = 34.7, so 35 boards.

Frequently Asked Questions

What gap should I leave between deck boards?

Leave 1/8 to 1/4 inch between boards so water drains and the wood can expand. Wider gaps suit wet climates.

How many screws per deck board?

Two screws at every joist. With joists 16 inches apart, a 12 ft board needs about 18 screws.

Should deck boards run long or short?

Run boards along the shorter deck dimension when you can, so you use fewer boards and have fewer joints.

How much extra decking should I buy?

Buy 10% extra for cuts and bad boards, and 15% if the deck has angles or a picture-frame border.

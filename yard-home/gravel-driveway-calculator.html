How It Works

Formula: Cubic yards = length (ft) × width (ft) × depth (in) ÷ 12 ÷ 27. Tons = cubic yards × 1.4, because one cubic yard of gravel weighs about 1.4 tons.

Worked example: A driveway 40 ft long, 12 ft wide and 4 in deep: 40 × 12 × 4 ÷ 12 = 160 cubic feet. 160 ÷ 27 = 5.93 cubic yards. Add 10% waste: 5.93 × 1.1 = 6.52 cubic yards. 6.52 × 1.4 = 9.13 tons of gravel.

Frequently Asked Questions

How deep should a gravel driveway be?

Four inches of gravel over a compacted base works for most cars. Use six inches if heavy trucks use the driveway.

How many tons of gravel do I need?

Multiply your cubic yards by 1.4. A typical two-car driveway 40 ft by 12 ft at 4 inches deep needs about 9 tons.

Should I add extra for waste?

Yes, add 10% for settling, spreading unevenly and edges. This calculator adds it for you.

What size gravel is best for a driveway?

A 3/4 inch crushed stone locks together well and drains. Pea gravel looks nice but shifts under tyres.

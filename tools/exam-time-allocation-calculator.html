How It Works

Formula: Minutes per question = (Total exam minutes − Reserve minutes) ÷ Number of questions. To show minutes and seconds, take the whole minutes and multiply the decimal part by 60 to get seconds.

Worked example: A 180-minute exam has 60 questions and you reserve 15 minutes for reading and checking. Time for questions: 180 − 15 = 165 minutes. Minutes per question: 165 ÷ 60 = 2.75 minutes. The decimal 0.75 × 60 = 45 seconds, so you get 2 minutes 45 seconds per question.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much time should I spend per question?

Divide your available minutes by the number of questions. First set aside time for reading the paper and checking answers at the end, then split what is left evenly across the questions.

How much time should I reserve for checking?

Reserve about 10% of the exam time, or at least 10 to 15 minutes. Checking catches careless mistakes and is one of the easiest ways to gain extra marks.

Should every question get the same time?

No. Give more time to questions worth more marks. If a question is worth double the marks of another, it deserves about double the time.

What if I get stuck on a question?

Move on when your time for that question is up. Answer the questions you know first, then return to the hard ones with whatever time is left.

How It Works

Formula: Cumulative GPA = (current GPA × completed credits + new GPA × new credits) ÷ (completed credits + new credits). Each GPA is weighted by the credits behind it.

Worked example: Your GPA is 3.2 over 30 credits and your new semester GPA is 3.8 over 15 credits. Grade points so far: 3.2 × 30 = 96. New grade points: 3.8 × 15 = 57. Total points: 96 + 57 = 153 over 30 + 15 = 45 credits. 153 ÷ 45 = 3.40. Your new cumulative GPA is 3.40.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between cumulative GPA and semester GPA?

A semester GPA covers one term only. A cumulative GPA covers everything you have done so far, all semesters combined. Each new semester moves your cumulative GPA a little.

Can my cumulative GPA go down after a good semester?

Yes, if your new semester GPA is lower than your current cumulative GPA. A good semester only raises your cumulative GPA when it beats your average so far.

How many credits do I need to raise my cumulative GPA?

It depends on how many credits you already have. The more credits behind you, the slower your GPA moves. Use our credits to raise GPA calculator to find the exact number.

Does retaking a course change my cumulative GPA?

At most schools, yes. The new grade usually replaces the old one in your GPA. Use our retake GPA calculator to see the change before you decide.

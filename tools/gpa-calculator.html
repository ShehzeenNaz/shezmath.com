How It Works

Formula: GPA = total grade points ÷ total credits. Each course earns grade points equal to its grade value (A = 4, B = 3, C = 2, D = 1, F = 0) multiplied by its credit hours.

Worked example: Four courses: A (3 credits), B (3 credits), A (4 credits) and C (3 credits). Grade points are 4 × 3 = 12, 3 × 3 = 9, 4 × 4 = 16 and 2 × 3 = 6. That is 12 + 9 + 16 + 6 = 43 grade points over 3 + 3 + 4 + 3 = 13 credits. 43 ÷ 13 = 3.31. Your GPA is 3.31.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a 4.0 GPA scale mean?

On a 4.0 scale, an A is worth 4 points, a B is worth 3, a C is worth 2, a D is worth 1 and an F is worth 0. Your GPA is the average of these points, weighted by credit hours.

Do plus and minus grades count?

Many schools do count them, for example an A- is often 3.7 and a B+ is 3.3. This calculator uses whole letter grades only. If your school uses plus and minus grades, check its grade point table.

Can I calculate a GPA for more than four courses?

Yes. Work out the GPA for four courses at a time, then use the cumulative GPA calculator to combine the results. Or add up all your grade points and divide by your total credits.

What is the difference between GPA and weighted GPA?

A regular GPA caps every course at 4.0. A weighted GPA adds bonus points for harder courses, so an A in an AP class can be worth 5.0. Use our weighted GPA calculator for that.

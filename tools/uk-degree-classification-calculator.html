UK Degree Classification Calculator

Work out your UK degree class from your module marks. Enter your Year 2 and Year 3 averages with their weights to see if you get a First, 2:1, 2:2 or Third.

How It Works

Formula: Overall mark = (Year 2 mark × Year 2 weight ÷ 100) + (Year 3 mark × Year 3 weight ÷ 100). Boundaries: 70%+ First, 60–69% Upper Second (2:1), 50–59% Lower Second (2:2), 40–49% Third, below 40% Fail.

Worked example: Your Year 2 average is 65% with a 40% weight, and your Year 3 average is 72% with a 60% weight. First, 65 × 0.40 = 26. Then, 72 × 0.60 = 43.2. Add them: 26 + 43.2 = 69.2. Your overall mark is 69.2%, which is an Upper Second (2:1), just 0.8 marks away from a First.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is a UK degree classification worked out?

Universities combine your marks from each year using year weights, often 40% for Year 2 and 60% for Year 3. The weighted average then gives your class: 70% or more is a First, 60 to 69% is an Upper Second (2:1), 50 to 59% is a Lower Second (2:2), 40 to 49% is a Third, and below 40% is a Fail.

What mark do I need for a First?

You need a weighted average of 70% or more across the years that count toward your degree. For example, with Year 2 at 65% (40%) and Year 3 at 72% (60%), the average is 69.2%, which is 0.8 marks short of a First.

Does Year 1 count toward my degree classification?

At most UK universities Year 1 does not count toward the final classification, but you usually need to pass it to progress. Check your university handbook, as a few courses weight Year 1 as well.

What is the difference between a 2:1 and a 2:2?

A 2:1, or Upper Second, needs a weighted average of 60 to 69%, while a 2:2, or Lower Second, needs 50 to 59%. Many graduate jobs ask for a 2:1 as a minimum.

Dissertation Weighting Calculator

See how much your dissertation moves your final mark. Enter your mark without the dissertation, your dissertation mark and its weight to find the change.

Your numbers never leave your device. All calculations happen in your browser.

How It Works

Formula: Final mark = mark without dissertation × (100 − dissertation weight) ÷ 100 + dissertation mark × dissertation weight ÷ 100. Change = final mark − mark without dissertation.

Worked example: Your mark without the dissertation is 62%, your dissertation mark is 75%, and the dissertation is worth 25%. First, 62 × 0.75 = 46.5. Then, 75 × 0.25 = 18.75. Add them: 46.5 + 18.75 = 65.25. Your final mark is 65.25%, which is 3.25 points higher than without the dissertation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does a dissertation affect my final mark?

It depends on its weight. A dissertation worth 25% of your mark moves your final result by a quarter of the gap between your dissertation mark and your other marks. For example, 62% without the dissertation and 75% on a 25% dissertation gives a final mark of 65.25%, a rise of 3.25 points.

Can a dissertation lower my final mark?

Yes. If your dissertation mark is lower than your mark on the rest of your work, it will pull your final mark down. The bigger the dissertation weight, the bigger the fall.

What is the usual weight of a dissertation?

Most UK dissertations are worth 20 to 40 credits, which is often 25% of the final year or around 15 to 20% of the whole degree. Check your course handbook for the exact weight.

What dissertation mark do I need to raise my final mark by 5 points?

Divide the rise you want by the dissertation weight. For a 5-point rise with a 25% dissertation, you need a dissertation mark 20 points above your other marks, because 20 times 0.25 is 5.

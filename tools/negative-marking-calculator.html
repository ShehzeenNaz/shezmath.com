How It Works

Formula: Final score = (Correct answers × Marks per correct answer) − (Wrong answers × Negative marks per wrong answer). Unattempted questions score 0. Maximum possible score = Total questions × Marks per correct answer.

Worked example: An exam has 100 questions, with +1 per correct answer and −0.25 per wrong answer. You got 70 correct, 20 wrong, and left 10 unattempted. Correct marks: 70 × 1 = 70. Penalty: 20 × 0.25 = 5. Final score: 70 − 5 = 65 out of a maximum of 100, which is 65%.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is negative marking?

Negative marking means you lose marks for a wrong answer. For example, with +1 for a correct answer and -0.25 for a wrong answer, a guess that is wrong costs you a quarter of a mark.

Should I guess on a negative marking exam?

Only guess if you can rule out at least one option. With -0.25 per wrong answer and 4 options, a random guess breaks even on average, so blind guessing does not help or hurt over many questions.

Do unattempted questions lose marks?

No. Unattempted questions score 0. You only lose marks for answers you got wrong, so leaving a question blank is safer than a wild guess.

Can my score go below zero with negative marking?

In theory yes, if you get far more wrong than right. In practice most exams set the minimum score to zero, so a very low raw score is simply recorded as 0.

How GPA Works

GPA stands for Grade Point Average. It is a single number that sums up your grades. This guide explains the scale, the maths behind it, and how to raise it.

What Is GPA?

GPA is the average of your grades, turned into points. Each letter grade has a point value. Your GPA is the weighted average of those points. It gives schools, employers and universities a quick way to compare students. A high GPA shows steady strong grades. A low GPA shows weak or uneven grades. GPA is used most in the US and Canada. Many other countries use percentages instead. If you study in the UK, your degree class matters more than a GPA. But if you apply to a US university, you will almost always need a GPA.

The 4.0 Scale

The standard scale runs from 0 to 4.0. An A is worth 4.0 points. A B is worth 3.0. A C is worth 2.0. A D is worth 1.0. An F is worth 0. Most schools add plus and minus grades. An A minus is 3.7. A B plus is 3.3. A B minus is 2.7. A C plus is 2.3. The exact values can differ slightly by school. Some schools do not use plus and minus at all. A GPA of 4.0 is perfect. A GPA of 3.5 or above is excellent. A GPA of 3.0 is a B average and is solid. A GPA of 2.0 is a C average and is the minimum to graduate at many schools. A GPA below 2.0 puts you at risk of academic probation.

How Credits Weight Your Courses

Not all courses count the same. Each course has credits, also called credit hours. A course worth 4 credits counts twice as much as a course worth 2 credits. To find your GPA, multiply each grade point by the course credits. Add up all those numbers. Then divide by the total credits. Here is a small example. You take a 4-credit course and get an A, worth 4.0 points. That gives 16 grade points. You take a 2-credit course and get a B, worth 3.0 points. That gives 6 grade points. Your total is 22 grade points over 6 credits. Your GPA is 22 divided by 6, which is 3.67. The A counted more because the course had more credits. This is why a bad grade in a heavy course hurts more than a bad grade in a light course.

Weighted vs Unweighted GPA

Unweighted GPA uses the plain 4.0 scale. An A is 4.0, no matter how hard the course was. Weighted GPA gives extra points for harder courses. In an honours or AP course, an A might be worth 5.0 instead of 4.0. A B in that course might be worth 4.0. Weighted GPA can go above 4.0. A student with a 4.3 weighted GPA took hard courses and did well in them. Colleges often look at both numbers. Weighted GPA rewards students who challenge themselves. Unweighted GPA makes it easy to compare students fairly. If your school reports both, check which one a college asks for. Never report a weighted GPA as unweighted. That is a common mistake on applications.

Cumulative vs Semester GPA

Semester GPA covers one term only. It shows how you did in that term. Cumulative GPA covers all your terms so far. It is your overall GPA. Your cumulative GPA is what appears on your transcript. It moves slowly because it includes many courses. One bad semester will lower it, but it will not destroy it. One great semester will raise it, but only a little. The more credits you have, the slower your cumulative GPA moves. A first-year student can change their GPA fast. A final-year student can barely move it. This is why early grades matter so much. They set the base for everything that follows.

How to Raise Your GPA

First, focus on your heaviest courses. A good grade in a 4-credit course moves your GPA more than a good grade in a 1-credit course. Second, retake courses you failed or did poorly in, if your school allows it. Some schools replace the old grade. Others average the two grades. Check your school's retake policy before you decide. Third, take extra credits in subjects you are strong in. More good grades pull your average up. Fourth, talk to your teachers early. Ask what you need to improve. Small changes in study habits can lift a B to an A. Fifth, protect your GPA from new damage. Do not overload yourself with too many hard courses in one term. A realistic schedule beats an ambitious one that ends in low grades. Raising a GPA takes time. Set a target GPA for next term and work out the grades you need to reach it.

Grading Across the US, UK & Canada

The same score can mean very different things in different countries. This guide explains how grading works in the US, the UK and Canada, so you can read your results with confidence.

How Grading Works in the US

US schools and universities use letter grades on a 4.0 GPA scale. An A is worth 4.0 points. A B is worth 3.0. A C is worth 2.0. A D is worth 1.0. An F is worth 0. Most schools use plus and minus grades too. An A minus is usually 3.7. A B plus is usually 3.3. Your GPA is the average of your grade points, weighted by course credits. A course worth 4 credits counts more than a course worth 2 credits. A GPA of 3.5 or above is strong. A GPA of 3.0 is a solid B average. Many graduate programs want a GPA of 3.0 or higher. Percentages still exist in the US. A score of 90 to 100% is usually an A. A score of 80 to 89% is usually a B. A score of 70 to 79% is usually a C. A score below 60% is usually a fail. The exact cutoffs vary by school, so always check your own course syllabus.

How Grading Works in the UK

UK universities grade in percentages, not letters. A mark of 70% or above is a First Class degree, the highest class. A mark of 60 to 69% is an Upper Second Class degree, called a 2:1. A mark of 50 to 59% is a Lower Second Class degree, called a 2:2. A mark of 40 to 49% is a Third Class degree. Below 40% is a fail. UK percentages are much tougher than US percentages. A mark of 70% in the UK is excellent. A mark of 80% is rare. A mark of 90% is almost unheard of. This surprises many international students. A 65% in the UK is a good 2:1, while a 65% in the US would be a D. Most UK degrees weight the later years more heavily. Year 1 often counts for nothing. Year 2 might count for 40%. Year 3 might count for 60%. Your university handbook states the exact split. UK school grades use letters too. GCSE grades run from 9 down to 1. Grade 9 is the top mark. Grade 4 is a standard pass. A levels use A star down to E. An A star is the top grade.

How Grading Works in Canada

Canada has no single national grading system. Each province sets its own rules. Most Canadian universities use a mix of percentages, letter grades and GPA. Ontario high schools grade in percentages. A mark of 80 to 100% is an A. A mark of 70 to 79% is a B. A mark of 60 to 69% is a C. A mark of 50 to 59% is a D. Below 50% is a fail. British Columbia uses a similar percentage scale. Alberta also uses percentages, with 80% and above as an A. At university level, many Canadian schools use a 4.0 GPA scale like the US. Others use a 4.33 scale. Some use a 12-point scale. McMaster University, for example, uses a 12-point scale where 12 is the top mark. This mix can confuse students who move between provinces. Always convert your grades using your own university's official scale. Never guess the conversion when you apply for jobs or further study.

Converting Between Systems

Converting grades between countries is tricky. A 70% in the UK is a First, but a 70% in the US is a C. A 3.0 GPA in the US is a B average. The same GPA in Canada means roughly the same thing. But a UK 2:1 does not map neatly to a single GPA number. Most conversion guides place a 2:1 at around 3.3 to 3.7. A First maps to around 3.7 to 4.0. These are rough guides only. Universities that receive international applications use their own conversion tables. If you are applying abroad, ask the admissions office which scale they use. Do not convert your grades yourself on an application unless they ask you to. When in doubt, report your original marks and let the university convert them.
